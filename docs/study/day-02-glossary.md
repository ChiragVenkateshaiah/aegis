# Day 02 — SME Credit Underwriting Glossary

**Source:** OCC Comptroller's Handbook (Commercial Loans; Rating Credit Risk), FDIC Risk Management Manual
**Date studied:** 2026-05-21
**Reconstructed:** 2026-05-28

25 terms. Each entry includes a one-paragraph definition and an OCC/regulatory source citation where applicable.

---

## 1. UCA Cash Flow (Uniform Credit Analysis)

A cash-flow spreading methodology in which the analyst reclassifies a borrower's income statement into actual cash receipts and disbursements, bypassing accrual adjustments. The output is a four-line waterfall: Cash After Operations → Cash After Debt Service → Cash After Distributions → Net Cash Position. UCA is the OCC-preferred format because it surfaces negative operating cash flow that net income may hide.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Commercial Loans, "Analyzing Financial Statements"*

---

## 2. DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio)

Cash available to service debt divided by required annual debt service (principal + interest). Formula: `DSCR = Cash After Operations ÷ Annual P&I`. A DSCR below 1.00x means the borrower cannot service debt from operations. Most SME lenders require a minimum DSCR of 1.20x–1.25x; the OCC expects the policy threshold to be documented.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Commercial Loans, "Cash Flow Analysis"*

---

## 3. Global DSCR

An extension of DSCR that aggregates the borrower's business cash flow with the personal cash flow of the guarantor(s), then divides by total debt obligations (business + personal). Required when personal guarantors are a material repayment source. Formula: `Global DSCR = (CAO + guarantor personal income) ÷ (business debt service + personal obligations)`.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Commercial Loans, "Guarantor Analysis"*

---

## 4. BRR (Borrower Risk Rating)

An internal credit grade assigned to a borrower entity that estimates the borrower's Probability of Default (PD) independent of any specific loan facility. BRR drives the interagency classification bucket (Pass / Special Mention / Substandard / Doubtful / Loss). Under dual risk rating, BRR and FRR are maintained separately.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Rating Credit Risk*

---

## 5. FRR (Facility Risk Rating)

An internal credit grade assigned to a specific loan facility that estimates the Loss Given Default (LGD) — i.e., how much of the exposure would be lost if the borrower defaulted, after recoveries from collateral and guarantees. A well-collateralized facility can have a better FRR than its borrower's BRR.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Rating Credit Risk*

---

## 6. PD (Probability of Default)

The estimated likelihood that a borrower will default over a given time horizon (typically 1 year). PD is the primary driver of the BRR. Under Basel II/III internal ratings-based (IRB) approaches, PD is an input to regulatory capital calculations. For community banks, PD is often implied by the risk grade rather than modeled explicitly.

*Source: Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, International Convergence of Capital Measurement (Basel II), §271*

---

## 7. LGD (Loss Given Default)

The percentage of exposure expected to be lost if a default occurs, after recovery of collateral and enforcement of guarantees. LGD = 1 − Recovery Rate. LGD is driven primarily by collateral quality, seniority of lien, and guarantor strength. LGD is the primary driver of the FRR.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Rating Credit Risk, "Loss Given Default"*

---

## 8. EAD (Exposure at Default)

The outstanding loan balance (including drawn and undrawn commitments on revolvers) at the time of default. For a fully drawn term loan, EAD ≈ outstanding principal. For a revolver, EAD must account for expected drawdown behavior at default, which is typically higher than current utilization.

*Source: Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, Basel II §308–316*

---

## 9. Pass

The interagency classification for loans that do not exhibit any of the characteristics of criticized assets. Pass-grade loans are considered to present no more than normal and acceptable risk. OCC examiners expect Pass loans to show adequate debt service coverage, acceptable collateral, and sound management.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Rating Credit Risk, "Interagency Rating Definitions"*

---

## 10. Special Mention

The first criticized-but-not-classified category in the interagency system. A Special Mention loan has a potential weakness that, if left uncorrected, may result in deterioration of repayment prospects. It does not yet rise to Substandard because the weakness is not yet well-defined. Triggers for Special Mention include covenant violations, declining trends, or management concerns without current impairment.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Rating Credit Risk; Interagency Guidelines for Credit Risk Review, 2006*

---

## 11. Substandard

Classified asset — a loan that is inadequately protected by the current net worth and paying capacity of the borrower. Substandard loans have well-defined weaknesses that jeopardize repayment. The distinct possibility of loss is present but not imminent. A loan is typically moved to Substandard when DSCR falls below 1.00x or when the borrower is past due 90+ days.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Rating Credit Risk*

---

## 12. Doubtful

Classified asset — all weaknesses inherent in Substandard plus the added characteristic that collection or liquidation in full, on the basis of current facts, is highly questionable and improbable. Reserve for credit loss (ACL) is typically 50% or higher for Doubtful loans.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Rating Credit Risk*

---

## 13. Loss

Classified asset — loans considered uncollectible and of such little value that continuance as bankable assets is not warranted. A Loss classification does not mean the asset has absolutely no recovery value; it means the bank should not carry the asset at its book value. Loss loans are typically charged off in the period classified.

*Source: OCC Comptroller's Handbook, Rating Credit Risk*

---

## 14. Criticized Asset

Collective term for any asset classified Special Mention, Substandard, Doubtful, or Loss. Banks report criticized loan totals to examiners and to their boards. A rising criticized asset ratio is a leading indicator of credit quality deterioration.

*Source: OCC Uniform Agreement on the Classification of Assets, 2004*

---

## 15. Financial Covenant

A contractual obligation requiring the borrower to maintain a specified financial metric (ratio, balance, or coverage) at all times or as tested at defined intervals. Failure is an Event of Default or, for maintenance covenants, a covenant violation requiring waiver or amendment. Examples: minimum DSCR ≥ 1.20x, maximum leverage ≤ 4.0x, minimum liquidity ≥ $100K.

---

## 16. Reporting Covenant

A contractual obligation specifying the financial statements and other information the borrower must deliver to the lender, and the deadlines for delivery. Example: annual CPA-audited financials within 120 days of fiscal year end; quarterly internally-prepared financials within 45 days; immediate notice of any material adverse change.

---

## 17. Affirmative Covenant

A contractual obligation specifying actions the borrower must take. Examples: maintain all required business licenses; maintain property and liability insurance naming the bank as additional insured; pay all taxes when due; allow lender site visits on reasonable notice.

---

## 18. Negative Covenant

A contractual obligation specifying actions the borrower must not take without lender consent. Examples: no additional indebtedness above a threshold; no asset sales outside the ordinary course; no change of ownership exceeding 20%; no additional liens on pledged collateral.

---

## 19. Advance Rate

The percentage of an asset's appraised or book value that a lender will lend against that asset. Advance rates represent the lender's haircut for imperfect liquidation. Policy advance rates vary by collateral type: 75–80% for CRE, 80% for new equipment, 50–60% for used equipment, 80% for eligible AR, 40–50% for inventory. The resulting collateral coverage = appraised value × advance rate.

---

## 20. OLV (Orderly Liquidation Value)

The value a piece of equipment or real property would realize in a reasonable exposure period (typically 6–12 months) under no compulsion to sell, marketed to an appropriate audience. OLV is the standard basis for equipment advance rates on term loans.

*Source: USPAP (Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice); ASA Equipment Valuation Standards*

---

## 21. FLV (Forced Liquidation Value)

The value realizable in an immediate or near-immediate sale, often at auction with limited marketing time. FLV is typically 30–60% of OLV for equipment. Lenders may use FLV as the basis for advance rates on high-risk credits or distressed collateral.

*Source: USPAP; ASA Equipment Valuation Standards*

---

## 22. EBITDA

Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. A proxy for operating cash generation frequently used in covenant calculations and leverage ratios. Note: EBITDA is not UCA cash flow — it excludes working capital changes, capital expenditures, and owner distributions. Over-reliance on EBITDA can mask cash flow problems.

---

## 23. Working Capital

Current assets minus current liabilities. A measure of short-term liquidity. In the context of revolving credit, working capital determines the borrowing base. A declining working capital trend is an early warning signal for liquidity stress.

---

## 24. Tangible Net Worth (TNW)

Total equity minus intangible assets (goodwill, non-compete agreements, customer lists, patents). Used as a covenant metric because intangibles have no liquidation value. Minimum TNW covenants protect the lender from the borrower writing up goodwill to mask balance sheet deterioration.

---

## 25. ACL (Allowance for Credit Losses)

The reserve an institution holds against expected credit losses on its loan portfolio, under FASB ASC 326 (CECL — Current Expected Credit Loss methodology, effective 2020+). For community banks, ACL replaces the prior ALLL (Allowance for Loan and Lease Losses). ACL is calculated at the facility level and driven by PD × LGD × EAD, adjusted for the remaining contractual life of the loan.

*Source: FASB ASC 326-20; OCC Bulletin 2019-17 (CECL)*
